AI video tools make it tempting to produce content at scale. Type a prompt, get a presenter, publish twenty videos a week. Several brands we've spoken to this year have tried it.

What we keep hearing back is that it isn't working. One publisher told us its AI-generated videos were struggling on TikTok, and the team wasn't sure whether to keep going or switch back to people. That's the question this post answers, with our view on it.

## Why brands are trying AI video

It's easy to see the appeal. AI video is fast and cheap per video, it never needs casting or a filming day, and it promises volume, which matters when the advice is to post several times a week.

The trouble is that volume was never the hard part of short-form. The hard part is making something people want to watch to the end and send to a friend.

## What short-form platforms reward

On TikTok, Reels and Shorts, the algorithm pushes videos that keep people watching and get shared. Those two signals decide whether a video reaches beyond your existing followers.

People share content for three reasons. It's polarising, it's genuinely useful, or it's relatable. Two of those three depend on people. A hot take only lands if a real person is saying it. A relatable story only works if the viewer believes it happened.

## Why human-led content still wins

**Real reactions can't be generated.** The reason [street interviews](/street-interviews) work is that the answers are unpredictable. Someone hesitates, laughs, says something nobody expected. That's what makes people watch to the end. A generated answer is, by definition, the expected one.

**A face people recognise builds a following.** Organic growth comes from people recognising the same person in the same format, week after week. That only works if there's a person to recognise. See [brand videos without your founder on camera](/blog/social-content-without-founder-on-camera) for how a consistent creator does that job.

**Trust is the whole game in some categories.** If you're asking someone to download an app, move their savings or try a new product, they need to believe the person talking. Viewers are getting quicker at spotting generated video, and once they spot it, they stop trusting it.

**Shares are personal.** When someone sends a video to a friend, they're putting their name to it. People are less likely to do that with something that feels manufactured.

## Where AI tools can genuinely help

None of this means AI has no place in content. AI tools can speed up the work around the video: researching what your audience is searching for, sorting through comments to find the questions people keep asking, or roughing out ideas to react to.

But the part the viewer sees, the face, the voice, the reaction, should be human. That's where the watch time and the shares come from.

## Our view

We cast real people from a network of 300+ vetted UK creators, not AI presenters. That's a deliberate choice, and it's based on what we see working on our clients' accounts, not on a principle.

If you're testing AI video, judge it the same way you'd judge any format: by the share of viewers who watch to the end and by how often it's shared, not by how many videos you managed to post. If it can't compete on those, the cost saving isn't a saving.

## Quick answers

**Does AI-generated content work on TikTok?**
In our experience, it struggles. TikTok rewards watch time and shares, and people share content that feels real, such as genuine reactions, relatable stories and faces they recognise.

**Is AI video or human-led content better for brands?**
Human-led content, for anything that needs to build a following or earn trust. AI tools are more useful behind the scenes than on camera.

**Can AI help with social media content at all?**
Yes, around the video: research, finding the questions your audience asks and roughing out ideas. The part people watch works best with real people.

**Are your creators real people or AI?**
Real people. We cast from a network of 300+ vetted UK creators.
