If you market a financial product in the UK, any TikTok, Reel or Short you post can be a financial promotion. The FCA's rules don't relax because a video is short, funny or made by a creator. They do change how you plan it.

This is how the rules apply to short-form content, based on the FCA's own guidance, and how we build them into the work for mortgage and money brands. It isn't legal advice. Your compliance team, or the authorised firm that approves your promotions, has the final word.

## What counts as a financial promotion

Under [section 21 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/21), a business can't communicate an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity unless it is authorised, or an authorised firm has approved the content. Breaking that rule is a criminal offence.

On social media the net is wide. The FCA's social media guidance, [FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/finalised-guidance/fg24-1-finalised-guidance-financial-promotions-social-media), says any type of communication can be a financial promotion, memes included.

Since February 2024, an authorised firm also needs [FCA permission to approve promotions](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps23-13-introducing-gateway-firms-who-approve-financial-promotions) for businesses that aren't authorised. Its own promotions, its appointed representatives' and its group's are exempt.

## Each post has to stand on its own

This is the rule that changes short-form most. FG24/1 says every communication has to comply on its own. A link to the full terms is fine, but the post itself must already be fair, clear and not misleading.

For short-form video, that means:

- Key information hidden behind "see more" doesn't count as prominent. If it matters, it goes in the video or the visible part of the caption.
- In the FCA's short-form video examples, a required risk warning stays prominent on screen throughout. A warning that only appears in the caption isn't enough.
- The benefits shouldn't get more prominence than the risks, whether in on-screen text or in what someone says.
- On carousels and Stories, a required warning goes on every slide or frame.
- For high-risk investments such as crypto, the risk warning has to show for the whole promotion and can't be cut short.

Character limits aren't an excuse either. The FCA asks firms to consider whether social media suits a complex product at all.

## Creators and finfluencers: the promotion is still yours

If you pay a creator to talk about your product, you're still responsible. FG24/1 says firms remain responsible for promotions they cause to be made, and should monitor the people making them. A creator who promotes a financial product without approval can be committing a criminal offence themselves. Paid content also has to be labelled as an ad upfront, under the ASA's rules.

The FCA enforces this. In 2025 it led an [international crackdown on illegal finfluencers](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-leads-international-crackdown-illegal-finfluencers), with arrests and charges in the UK. In February 2026, [seven influencers were fined](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/influencers-fined-issuing-unauthorised-financial-promotions) after pleading guilty to promoting an unauthorised trading scheme to a combined 4.5 million Instagram followers.

It isn't only influencers. In 2024 the FCA had [19,766 promotions amended or withdrawn](https://www.fca.org.uk/data/financial-promotions-data-2024) after it stepped in with authorised firms, nearly double the year before.

## Consumer Duty raises the bar

The Consumer Duty has applied since July 2023. Its [consumer understanding outcome](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/prin2a/prin2as5) covers financial promotions on any channel, social media included. Communications have to be likely to be understood and help people make properly informed decisions, and firms should tailor them to the channel they use.

FG24/1 is clear that being fair, clear and not misleading isn't enough on its own to meet the Duty.

## What this means for your content plan

None of this rules short-form out. It changes what you make and how it gets signed off.

**Choose formats that don't lean on claims.** Street interviews are one way to stay inside the lines, because the video is people answering a question rather than the brand reading a claim. Street interviews took [Habito](/case-studies/habito), a digital mortgage broker, to 25 million organic views in 8 months with zero ad spend.

**Write the guardrails into the brief.** Phrases you can't use, statements you can't make and your disclaimer lines go in before anyone writes a script, not at the edit.

**Put warnings in the video, not just the caption.** Plan where on-screen text sits so the platform's buttons and captions don't cover it, and keep it up for as long as it needs to be read.

**Sign off everything, then keep records.** Sign-off should come from someone competent and senior. FG24/1 also says not to rely on the platforms to keep your records, because posts can be deleted. Save the final file, the caption and the approval.

**Review again before a video becomes an ad.** A video that went out organically may need a longer disclaimer, or a line taken out, before it runs as a paid ad.

## Quick answers

**Is a TikTok a financial promotion?**
It can be. If it invites or encourages people to engage in investment activity, it can be a financial promotion under section 21, whatever the format. Memes and short videos count.

**Can the risk warning go in the caption?**
In the FCA's short-form video examples, a required risk warning stays prominent on screen throughout. A warning only in the caption, or hidden behind "see more", isn't enough.

**Are we responsible for what influencers say about our product?**
Yes, for promotions you cause to be made, including by creators you pay. Promoting a financial product without approval can also be a criminal offence for the influencer.

**Who can approve a financial promotion?**
An authorised firm. Since February 2024, a firm approving promotions for businesses that aren't authorised also needs FCA permission to do so.

**Does the Consumer Duty apply to social media?**
Yes. Its consumer understanding outcome covers financial promotions on social media, and being fair, clear and not misleading isn't enough on its own.
