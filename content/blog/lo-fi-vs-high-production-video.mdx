It's one of the most common debates we hear on discovery calls, and it usually happens inside the brand's own team. Someone senior wants polished, high-production video. The person running social says lo-fi performs better.

One marketing lead put it to us like this: "We do brilliant shoots with photographers and videographers, but it's very high production. And we don't really need that for these one-off posts that really are just for social."

Both sides are partly right. Here's how we think about it.

## What "native" really means

On TikTok, Reels and Shorts, people scroll past anything that looks like an advert. Content that looks like it belongs in the feed, made by a person rather than a brand, gets watched.

That's what "lo-fi" is really about. It isn't about cheap cameras or bad lighting. It's about a video that feels like the rest of the feed: a face talking to camera, a real reaction, a caption written the way people actually talk.

You're not competing with other brands' ads on the For You feed. You're competing with every creator your audience follows, and creators don't shoot like a brand campaign.

## When lo-fi wins

**Organic content.** On organic, the algorithm decides who sees your video, and it rewards watch time and shares. Native-looking content usually does better on both, because people don't skip it as an ad.

**Street interviews and storytime.** These formats only work if they feel real. A polished street interview looks staged, which is the opposite of what makes the format work.

**Speed.** Lo-fi and street interview content can go from the shoot to your content calendar in about 3 to 5 days, so you can still catch a trend or a news moment.

## When production earns its keep

High production isn't wrong. It's a tool for specific jobs.

**Premium brands with a strong visual identity.** If your brand world is your advantage, the content has to stay inside it.

**Some organic formats.** Our Organic Social Productions use locations, actors and scripts for podcast-style debates, transformations and explainers. They're more produced than a street interview, but they still follow the rules of the feed.

**Paid creative.** With paid, you've already bought the reach. The question is whether the viewer believes you and acts. Our Paid Social Productions are higher-fidelity concepts, such as scripted street interviews, podcast-style ads and reaction videos, and they still look native until the end.

## Lo-fi doesn't mean off-brand

The worry we hear from in-house teams is reasonable: will lo-fi content look cheap next to everything else the brand puts out? Another marketing lead described the challenge as "finding that balance between UGC and being more relatable and not being polished... but still within our brand world."

That's a production choice, not a compromise. We often film in 4K and edit for a native feel, so the footage is sharp but the video still feels like the feed. Where a brand wants something rawer, we can shoot entirely on an iPhone with a gimbal. Either way, your fonts, colours, tone of voice and talent styling come from your brand guidelines, and you approve every video before it's posted.

## Settle it with data, not opinions

Rather than argue about it, test it. Make the same idea in a lo-fi version and a higher-production version, post both, and let your audience decide. In our [70/20/10 rule](/blog/70-20-10-content-rule), that's exactly the kind of small variable we change one at a time.

Judge the test on watch time, not views. The number to look at is the share of viewers who watch the whole video. As a rule of thumb, we treat under 10% as a red flag, over 20% as a strong signal and 30% or more as the sweet spot. Then look at [shares](/blog/shares-beat-views), which tell you whether people thought it was worth passing on.

Whichever version wins becomes the default for that format, until the next test.

## Quick answers

**Does lo-fi content perform better than high production on social media?**
On organic, usually yes, because native-looking videos don't get skipped as adverts. On paid, higher production can work well, as long as the ad still looks like it belongs in the feed.

**Is lo-fi content bad for premium brands?**
No. Lo-fi is about the feel of the video, not the quality. Filming in 4K and editing for a native feel keeps the brand's styling while still fitting the feed.

**When is high production worth it on social?**
For brands whose visual identity is their advantage, for produced organic formats like podcast-style debates and transformations, and for paid creative that has to convince people to act.

**How do you decide between lo-fi and high production?**
Test the same idea both ways and compare the share of viewers who watch the whole video, plus shares. Let the results decide.

**What is a good watch-full-video rate?**
As a rule of thumb, over 20% is a strong signal and 30% or more is the sweet spot. Under 10% is a red flag.
