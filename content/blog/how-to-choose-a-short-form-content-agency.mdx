This is the guide we'd want if we were on your side of the table. It's honest about where we fit and where we don't.

## First, know what kind of agency you're looking at

"Short-form agency" covers very different businesses. Most fall into one of these:

**Influencer marketing agencies** match you with creators who post about you on *their* channels. Good for reach in a creator's audience. You don't usually build your own account, and you rent the attention rather than own it.

**Paid social agencies** focus on ad creative and media buying. Good if you already have an organic presence and want performance. Less useful if nobody is following you yet.

**Production houses** make high-quality video for a campaign or launch. Brilliant for one-off hero content, but not set up to post three times a week and learn from what lands.

**Freelancers and in-house hires** can work well, but one person rarely covers strategy, casting, filming, editing and posting, and you carry the management.

**Short-form content agencies** run the whole engine on *your* channels: strategy, creators, filming, editing and posting, often with paid creative alongside. This is what we do.

None of these is better in general. The right one depends on whether you want to build your own audience, buy performance, or make one big piece.

## The questions worth asking before you sign

**"Who's going to be on camera?"**
This is where most brands get stuck. Ask whether the agency has its own creator network, how it casts, and whether you'll be expected to appear yourselves.

**"Can I see results from a brand like mine?"**
Not just big view counts. Ask for a case study in your category or with a similar audience, with a starting point and a timeline. Views mean more when you know where they started.

**"Whose account does it go on, and who posts?"**
Make sure the content builds *your* channels and that you keep everything if you part ways.

**"How do you decide what to make next?"**
Good agencies test formats deliberately and double down on what works. Vague answers about "trends" are a warning sign.

**"How does sign-off work?"**
You should approve concepts before anything is filmed and see every edit before it goes live. For regulated brands, ask how disclaimers and compliance review fit in.

**"How quickly will we see content?"**
Weeks, not months. Ask what happens in the first month.

**"Can you work alongside our team?"**
If you have an in-house marketing team, you want an agency that slots in, not one that needs to own everything.

## Red flags

- **Guaranteed virality.** No one can promise a video will go viral. Agencies that do are selling the pitch, not the work.
- **Follower counts as the headline result.** Followers are easy to inflate and say little about business impact. Look for views per video, shares, and what happened to sign-ups or downloads.
- **One-size-fits-all formats.** If every client's content looks the same, yours will too.
- **No clear process for learning.** If they can't explain how they decide what to keep and what to drop, results will be luck.

## Why location matters (and when it doesn't)

Much of short-form content can be made remotely. But formats like street interviews need people on the ground, and filming in London needs a team that knows where to shoot and who to cast. An agency based where you are also makes shoots, strategy sessions and approvals easier. If your content is mostly remote UGC, location matters less.

## Quick answers

**What does a short-form content agency do?**
It plans, casts, films, edits and posts short videos for TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts on your brand's own channels, and usually learns from performance to decide what to make next.

**How is a short-form content agency different from an influencer agency?**
An influencer agency gets creators to post about you on their channels. A short-form content agency builds your own channels, often using creators on camera.

**How long before we see results?**
First content should go live within a few weeks. Meaningful growth usually takes a few months of consistent posting and testing.

**Should we choose an agency in London?**
If you want in-person formats like street interviews, or easy access to shoots and the team, yes. For remote-only content it matters less.
