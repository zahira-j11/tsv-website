Students are one of the most valuable audiences a brand can win, and one of the hardest. They're quick to scroll past anything that looks like an ad.

We've made short-form content for student brands including [Uni Compare](/case-studies/uni-compare), [TALAB](/case-studies/talab), Applicaa and Blackbullion. Here's what we've learned about reaching university students on TikTok, Reels and Shorts.

## Why most student marketing gets scrolled past

Most brands talk to students the way a prospectus does. Polished, cheerful, and clearly not made by anyone who's a student. A stock shot of people laughing on a lawn, then a discount code.

On TikTok, that loses in the first second. Students open the app to see people like them, talking about things they actually deal with. If a video looks like an ad, [they're gone before the brand name appears](/blog/ugc-ads-that-dont-look-like-ads).

The brands that win with students make content students would make themselves. The brand stays in the background until there's an audience to hear it.

## 1. Put real students on camera

The format that has worked best for our student clients is the [street interview](/street-interviews). A presenter stops students and asks a question they genuinely want to answer: how much rent they pay, how they chose their course, whether uni has been worth it so far.

It works for three reasons. The people on screen look like the viewer. The answers are real, so the comments fill up with students adding their own. And the brand owns the conversation without having to make claims.

For Uni Compare, a UK university comparison platform, street interviews and scripted skits about choosing a university passed 1 million views in the first 8 weeks. Average views per video went from under 1,000 to 83,000, and the content contributed to a significant rise in app downloads.

Where you film matters as much as what you ask. We usually film outside universities or wherever students actually spend time, because a street full of tourists won't give you student answers. Here's [how a street interview shoot works](/blog/how-a-street-interview-shoot-works), from the question to the edit.

## 2. Cast presenters who look like your audience

Students trust their peers more than any brand, so the person fronting the content matters as much as the script. We cast presenters and creators from our network who look and sound like the students you want to reach.

If you're running a recurring series, keep the same face. After a few weeks, students start to recognise the person, and the account stops feeling like a brand. It also means nobody from your team has to [be on camera](/blog/social-content-without-founder-on-camera).

## 3. Make content worth sending to a friend

Students share constantly, and [a share is one of the strongest signals](/blog/shares-beat-views) a video can get. The easiest way to earn one is to be useful.

We saw this one stage earlier, with GCSE students. For results day, we made a video for an edtech client listing every place giving away free food that day. It reached just under a million views and 28,000 shares, because students sent it to the friends they were going out with.

Lists work even better when they're long. For an edtech client, turning a "top 3 universities" video into a "top 40" took it from about 6,000 views to 10 million. The longer the list, the more students see their own university in it, and the more they tag their friends.

For university students, useful might mean:

- the cheapest places to eat near campus
- how to make the maintenance loan last until the end of term
- the best study spots in the city
- what to pack for halls, and what to leave at home

The test is simple: could you name the friend someone would send it to? If not, the idea goes back.

## 4. Plan around the student year

Students' attention follows a calendar. The big moments are when they're searching, posting and sharing most:

- **Results day and Clearing (August):** next year's first-years are choosing, panicking and celebrating.
- **Freshers (September and October):** a new city, new friends and a lot of first times.
- **The start of each term:** the student loan lands, and "how do I make this last?" follows soon after.
- **The house hunt:** many students start looking for next year's house well before the summer.
- **Exam season (May and June):** revision, stress and a lot of procrastinating.

We plan each month around what students are going through, and pitch concepts ahead of the big moments. That way you approve them before the day, not after. A results-day video posted a week late is a video about last week.

## 5. Keep the brand in the background, then bring it forward

When an account is new, most of the content should be built for discovery: relatable, useful, shareable. Once the account is posting consistently and the algorithm knows who to show it to, the brand can step forward with product moments and clearer calls to action.

TALAB, a student concierge app, came to us at launch with no audience. We started with street interviews about student life, not the app. That reached 1 million views in the first 5 weeks, and TALAB grew to 6,000 users, with organic content driving most of that growth.

Start with the hard sell and nobody shares it, so no new students see it. Start with content students want, and by the time the brand steps forward there's an audience listening.

## What to measure

Views tell you about reach. For student content, these tell you more:

- **Shares and saves:** whether students think it's worth sending or keeping.
- **Watched full video rate:** under 10% is a red flag, over 20% is a strong signal, and 30% or more is the sweet spot.
- **Comments:** whether students are joining in, tagging friends and arguing about the answer.
- **Downloads or sign-ups over a month, not per video:** organic content builds an audience over weeks, so judge it over weeks.

## Quick answers

**How do you market to university students on TikTok?**
Put real students on camera, make content they'd send to a friend, and plan around the student year. Keep the brand in the background until the account has an audience.

**What content do university students engage with most?**
In our experience: street interviews with students, long lists and rankings, and useful guides tied to something they're going through, like freshers or making the student loan last.

**When is the best time to market to students?**
The biggest moments are results day and Clearing in August, freshers in September and October, and the start of each term when the student loan lands. Plan concepts ahead so they're approved before the day.

**Do you need famous creators to reach students?**
No. Students respond to people who look like them. We cast presenters and creators from our network of 300+ vetted UK creators to match your audience.
