If you're spending steadily on Meta or TikTok, your creative is wearing out faster than you think. The same people see the same ad again and again, results slip while spend stays the same, and the only real fix is new creative.

A performance lead at a fintech brand summed up their situation on a call with us: "You need to refresh assets... on average between one to two weeks." The problem was their agency. "The SLA is minimum three weeks if there is no back and forth."

That gap, needing new ads every week or two and getting them every three weeks or more, is one of the most common reasons brands come to us. Here's how to close it.

## Why creative wears out

Paid social platforms show your ad to the people most likely to respond. Over time, those people have seen it. They've either acted already or decided not to, and showing them the same video again does less each time.

Fresh creative resets that. A new hook, a new face or a new angle on the same offer gives the platform something new to test and the viewer a reason to stop again.

## How often to refresh

There's no single number, because it depends on your spend, your audience size and how many ads you run at once. As a starting point, brands we speak to who spend steadily need fresh assets every one to two weeks. Smaller budgets reaching a big audience can go longer. Bigger budgets reaching a narrow audience need new creative sooner.

The signal to watch is your own results. When performance drops while spend and targeting stay the same, the creative is usually the reason.

## Signs your creative pipeline is too slow

- New ads arrive after the last batch has already worn out.
- Every batch needs several rounds of back and forth before it's usable.
- Each shoot produces one or two ads, not a set to test.
- Your team spends more time chasing creative than reading results.

## How we keep refreshes on time

Speed doesn't come from rushing the edit. It comes from planning.

**Plan the variations before the shoot.** We script hook variations and plan every aspect ratio (9:16, 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9) before filming, so one session produces a full test set rather than a single ad.

**Run two production cycles at once.** Paid creative usually arrives about 10 days after you approve the script. While one batch is being tested, the next is already in production, which means a fresh batch can land every fortnight.

**Change the right things.** A refresh doesn't always mean a new concept. We change hooks first, then the framing and who the ad seems to be talking to, then the format. Our post on [UGC ads that don't look like ads](/blog/ugc-ads-that-dont-look-like-ads) explains the hook and framing variations we rotate.

**Use both paid formats.** Remote Creator UGC keeps a steady flow of talking-to-camera ads. Paid Social Productions, such as scripted street interviews and podcast-style ads, add bigger concepts that look nothing like the rest of the category.

## Brief once, properly

Most delays happen after filming, when something that should have been in the brief comes up in review. The clearer the brief up front, the less back and forth later. A good paid brief covers:

- who the ad is for, and what stops them buying
- the must-say lines, claims and disclaimers, plus anything that can't be said
- the offer and the call to action
- examples of ads you like, and ones you don't

You approve the concepts and scripts before we film, and the final videos before they're delivered. If your team has its own sign-off process, we build our timeline around it.

## Feed the results back

Fresh creative only helps if you learn from the last batch. Every month, look at which hooks, framings and formats held up longest, and make more of those.
