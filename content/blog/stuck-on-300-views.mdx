I get this conversation at least twice a week. A brand has been posting for months, the videos look good, and every single one lands somewhere around 300 views. Or it was getting consistent views and then dropped off a cliff: 10k per video becomes 800, sometimes overnight, sometimes over two or three weeks. The panic sets in, the posting frequency goes up, and things usually get worse.

Before you change anything, you need to understand why it is happening. The fix depends entirely on the cause, and most of the advice online is either too vague to be useful or just wrong.

## Why so many videos stop at around 300 views

Short-form platforms do not show your video to your followers first. TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts all work in roughly the same way: they show a new video to a small test group of people whose interests match the content, then watch how that group responds.

What they are measuring is whether people keep watching. Do they get past the first few seconds? Do they watch to the end, or watch again? Do they share it, visit your profile, or leave a comment that took some thought?

If the test group responds well, the video goes to a bigger group, then a bigger one. That is how videos reach hundreds of thousands of views. If the test group scrolls past, distribution stops there. A few hundred views is what a video gets when it never makes it out of that first group.

So 300 views is not bad luck and it is not a shadowban. It is the platform telling you the first few seconds are not holding people. That also means it is fixable, and follower count has very little to do with it.

## Your opening doesn't stop the scroll

This is the most common reason videos stall at the first hurdle. Platforms measure how many viewers make it past the first few seconds, and if that number is low, nothing after it gets a chance.

Most branded content opens on the brand: a logo, a product shot, a polished intro. Viewers recognise the format as an ad and skip before they have consciously decided to. The videos that travel open on something the viewer cares about: a question, a surprising answer, a tension they want resolved.

The fix is to look at your analytics honestly. If people are leaving in the first three seconds, the hook is the problem, not the rest of the video. Test aggressively. Different opening lines, different first frames, different pacing. One strong hook can reset your baseline quickly.

## You drifted out of your niche

This is the most common reason views drop after a good run.

The algorithm builds a model of what your account is about from your posting history, and uses it to decide who to show your content to. When that model is accurate, your content reaches people likely to watch it, which produces good completion rates, which produces more distribution.

When you post outside your niche, you disrupt that model. The platform does not know who to show the new content to, so it tests it on a broader, less relevant group. Engagement tanks, the platform reads that as weak content and pulls back, often on your next posts too, even when they are back to your normal subject matter.

This is why chasing trending audio or topics outside your lane usually backfires. You might get a spike if the trend is big enough, but you pay for it in the weeks after.

The fix: go back to what worked and stay there for at least two to three weeks before deciding whether the recovery is working.

## You had a gap in posting

There is no official penalty for going quiet, but there is a practical effect. When you post consistently, the platform has fresh data about your account and knows who responds to it. When you stop, that data stops. When you start again, it treats you more like a new account while it rebuilds its picture of you.

The result looks like a penalty. It isn't quite, but it takes time to rebuild, and inconsistent posting makes it harder.

The fix: commit to a consistent cadence and stick to it even when individual posts underperform. The algorithm needs volume to work with.

## You got mislabelled

If your account posts content that could apply to more than one audience, the algorithm sometimes lands on the wrong one. A fitness brand that also posts lifestyle content can find its fitness videos going to a lifestyle audience that isn't interested. Engagement drops, the algorithm doubles down on that classification, and you end up in a loop.

Signs this is happening: your views come from people who don't match your target audience, your comments are full of people who seem confused about what you do, or your most-viewed videos are consistently your least representative ones.

The fix is deliberate. Post a run of your most specific content for two to four weeks. Don't try to appeal broadly. Give the algorithm unambiguous data about what your account is actually about.

## Something got flagged

Content review systems are imperfect. Health claims, certain product categories and anything touching regulated industries can sometimes have distribution throttled without any formal notification.

If you're in wellness, finance, food or another regulated space and your views dropped suddenly with no change in what you post, check your notifications for policy messages and look for videos with reduced distribution in your analytics. Sometimes removing or editing the flagged video is enough. Sometimes it takes a few weeks to recover regardless.

## The thing most brands do wrong

They panic and change everything at once.

New posting times, new formats, new topics, higher frequency. If any of those changes help, you won't know which one did it. If any of them hurt, you won't know which one to reverse.

Diagnose first. Make one change at a time. Give each change at least a week before drawing conclusions. Analytics lag enough that a three-day test tells you almost nothing.

Low views are almost always fixable. When we started with [Habito](/case-studies/habito), its videos were averaging around 1,000 views. Eight months later it had reached 25 million organic views with zero ad spend. [Uni Compare](/case-studies/uni-compare) went from under 1,000 views per video to an average of 83,000.

## Quick answers

**Why do my TikToks only get around 300 views?**
The platform shows every new video to a small test group first. If that group scrolls past in the first few seconds, distribution stops, and a few hundred views is usually where that happens. A stronger opening is the most common fix.

**Is 300 views a shadowban?**
Almost never. It is the normal result of a video not holding its first test audience. A shadowban or flagged content usually shows up as a sudden drop across all your videos, not a steady 300.

**Does follower count affect how many views I get?**
Much less than people think. Distribution is decided by how each video performs with its test group, so a small account with compelling content can outperform a large one.

**How long does it take to recover after views drop?**
Usually two to four weeks of consistent, on-niche posting, changing one thing at a time.
