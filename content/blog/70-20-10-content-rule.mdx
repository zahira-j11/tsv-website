On organic social, our goal for every client is the same. Find a format that gets views, then double down on it and post it consistently, so the feed knows exactly who you are and what you talk about. When it is working, a brand's page looks like it is running a few viral mini-series at once.

The 70/20/10 rule is how we plan each month to get there. 70% of the content is the formats that are already working, with small variables changed. 20% is about what we are actually saying inside those formats. 10% is new formats we have never tried.

## Why double down on one format?

Because of where people actually watch. On TikTok, Instagram and YouTube Shorts, most of what people see is served to them on the For You or Explore feed, not from accounts they follow. They are flicking through video after video, and even a viral one is easy to forget.

On that feed, you are not just competing with other brands. You are competing with creators, meme pages, celebrities and the news. The brands that win are the ones you recognise before you have read a word. Same format, same look, same kind of question, over and over. We describe it to clients as becoming the purple blocks in someone's scroll: they see it and they know it is you.

That only happens if you commit to a format. A brand that jumps on every new trend ends up with forty videos that have nothing to do with each other, and nobody can tell whose they are.

So the 70/20/10 rule is not about spreading your bets. It is about doubling down on what works while still learning how to make it work better.

## The 70: formats that have already spiked

Most of each month goes on the formats that have shown what we call micro spikes. More on those below. We keep making them, and in each batch we change small variables to see what lifts them further.

The variables are often tiny. For a finance app client, we run a format where a presenter reads out finance horror stories from Reddit. In the second month, we added a light glowing in the background, an idea we had seen on other storytime creators. Nothing else changed. The first video with the light got 20,000 views on Instagram Reels, and it has been in every one since.

With the same client we tested a male presenter against a female presenter, different font styles, music against no music, split screen against no split screen. One change at a time, so we know what made the difference.

This is the least glamorous part of the plan and the part that does most of the work. Some of our clients' best formats have been performing for over a year, and there is no reason to stop making them while they keep delivering.

## The 20: what we are actually saying

The next 20% looks at the meat of the content. The format stays the same, but we test what goes inside it: the topic, the question, the story, the angle.

A street interview format can run for months, but the questions change every shoot. A readout format stays the same, but the stories change. This is where we find out which subjects your audience cares about most, and those answers feed straight back into the 70%.

## The 10: new formats

The last 10% is experimentation. New formats we have not tried on that account yet. For the finance app, that has meant testing listicle styles and a podcast-style video with two people talking back and forth.

Most experiments will not beat the core format, and that is fine. The point is that when one does spike, it becomes the next series on the page. That is how an account goes from one recognisable format to several.

## How we decide what counts as working

We do not wait for a video to hit a million views before we take it seriously. We look for micro spikes: early videos that do noticeably better than the rest.

Take the account's average views. A video that doubles it is a signal. Five times the average is a strong signal. On a newer account, spikes of 10,000 to 50,000 views are exactly what we are looking for. They are small in the grand scheme of social, but they tell us which structure to double down on.

For a brand-new account, the first milestones are simpler. Get a format over 1,000 views, then over 10,000. Whatever gets you past 10,000 is what you build on.

Patience matters too. One video under 1,000 views does not mean a format is dead. We post a format six to twelve times before deciding whether it works, and the first three months with any new client are mostly about testing to find it.

Here is an example of why. In our first month with a mortgage client, one of the videos we made got around 1,000 views on TikTok and Instagram. On YouTube Shorts, the same video got 25,000. Their team asked whether we should keep it. We did. We shortened the captions and kept iterating, and treated the YouTube number as the signal it was. Signals like that are easy to miss if you only look at one platform or one video.

## Is it the same for paid?

No. On organic, the algorithm decides whether you deserve the views, so you go deep on one format and make it recognisable.

On paid, you are buying the reach, so the question becomes whether the ad converts. Each creative works on its own rather than building a feed, so we test a much wider range of styles and hooks. We still lean on proven winners, but we are not trying to make every ad look like the last one.

## Quick answers

**What is the 70/20/10 rule for social media content?**
70% of the content is formats that have already shown results, with small variables tested. 20% tests what you talk about inside those formats. 10% tries new formats.

**Should a brand stick to one content format?**
On organic, yes, once you have found one that works. Being recognisable in the feed is what turns views into a following. The 10% is how you find the next format.

**How long before you know if a format works?**
We give a format six to twelve posts before judging it. One weak video is not enough evidence.

**What counts as a winning video?**
Anything that does at least double the account's average views. On a new account, early spikes of 10,000 to 50,000 views are the signal to double down.
