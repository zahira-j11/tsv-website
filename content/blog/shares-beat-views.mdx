Views are the result, not the thing you can control. What you can control are the two signals that decide how far a video travels on TikTok, Reels and Shorts: whether people watch it through, and whether they share it. Of the two, shares are arguably the most important, and they are the one most brands never plan for.

## What a share actually does

Think about what happens when you send someone a video. They get a notification. Maybe they were not even on the app. They were cooking, or on another app, or on the train. The notification pulls them in to watch what you sent.

From the platform's point of view, that video just brought a person onto TikTok, Instagram or YouTube who was not there a minute ago. That is exactly what every platform wants more of. So the video that does it gets rewarded with more reach.

A like tells the platform someone enjoyed something. A share tells it the video is good enough to recruit new viewers. That is a very different level of signal.

## The video that proved it for us

One of our edtech clients speaks to GCSE students, so roughly 14 to 17 year olds. For GCSE results day we made a video that was simply a list: all the places you could get free food on results day. We made the list properly, not a lazy top five.

At the time we screenshotted it, the video had just under a million views and 28,000 shares. That is nearly three shares for every hundred views.

There was no trick in the edit. People sent it to their friends because it was useful to their friends. Students tagged each other in the comments to make plans. Every one of those shares put the video in front of someone new, and the platform kept pushing it.

## The three reasons people share

Nearly every video that gets shared does at least one of these things.

**It is useful.** Like the free food list. The viewer thinks, "my friend needs this", and sends it. Useful content also gets saved, which is another good signal.

**It is polarising.** Something that makes you react and want someone else's opinion. "This is crazy, have you seen this?" Debate formats work well here. A North versus South question gets people arguing in the comments and sending it to the friend from the other side.

**It is relatable.** The viewer sees themselves, or someone they know, in it. It is the "this is so you" video. Relatable content works best when it is specific. A video about "people" is rarely shared. A video about a very particular type of person gets sent to that person.

Sometimes a video does two of these at once. That is when things really travel.

## The other half: retention

Shares are not the only thing we look at. The other signal is retention: do people watch to the end?

The first second decides a lot. The viewer should know what is going on almost instantly. Game-style formats are good at this. If someone is picking between two cities, "Manchester or Leeds?", "Leeds or London?", you understand the video in under a second, and you want to stay to see which one they end up choosing. That curiosity gap is what keeps people watching.

A video with strong retention gets shown to more people. A video that also gets shared gets shown to people outside your usual audience. You want both.

## How we plan for shares

Before a concept gets approved, we ask one question: who would someone send this to?

If the answer is "no one in particular", the concept goes back. If the answer is a specific person ("my housemate who is terrible with money", "my friend who is moving to London"), it usually has a chance.

A few things we do because of that:

- We make content about a specific audience, not everyone. Specific gets shared. Broad gets scrolled past.
- We build lists and resources properly, so they are worth saving and sending.
- We leave room for a reaction. A question with a clear answer ends the conversation. A question that splits people starts one.
- We keep the brand in the background at first. Nobody shares an ad with their friends.

## Where the brand comes in

This does not mean brand content has to stay vague forever. We think about it as a funnel within the funnel.

When an account is new, most of the content sits at the top, built for discovery and shares. Once the account is posting consistently and the algorithm knows who to show it to, we start bringing the brand in more: product moments, clearer calls to action, content designed for people who already follow.

Start with the hard sell and nobody shares it, so nobody new sees it. Start with shareable content, and by the time the brand steps forward there is an audience to hear it.

## Quick answers

**Do shares matter more than likes on TikTok?**
In our experience, yes. A share can bring a new person onto the platform, which is why platforms reward it so strongly.

**What makes a brand video shareable?**
It has to be useful, polarising or relatable, ideally to a specific group of people. The test is whether you can name who someone would send it to.

**What is a good share rate?**
It varies a lot by format and audience. Our free food video for GCSE students got 28,000 shares on just under a million views, nearly 3%, which is unusually high. The more useful thing is to compare each video with your own account's average.

**Should every video be designed to be shared?**
Not every video, but most of the discovery content should be. Content for existing followers, like product explainers, can do a different job.
