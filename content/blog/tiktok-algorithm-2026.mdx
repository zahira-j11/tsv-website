We have produced hundreds of organic TikTok videos across brands in almost every category. The most common thing I hear from brands who come to us after failing on their own is some version of "we just don't understand the algorithm."

What they usually mean is: we posted content we thought was good and nothing happened.

The algorithm isn't mysterious. It is a distribution system with clear logic. Once you understand what it's actually optimising for, most of what feels random starts to make sense.

## What the algorithm is trying to do

TikTok's algorithm has one job: keep people on the app for as long as possible.

Every decision it makes is in service of that. It does not care about your brand. It does not care how much you spent on your video. It does not care that you have 200,000 followers. It cares about one thing: if it shows your content to a particular person right now, how likely are they to keep watching?

This sounds simple. The implications are significant.

## How distribution actually works

When you post a video, TikTok does not show it to your followers first. It shows it to a small test group, typically between a few hundred and a few thousand accounts, selected based on the characteristics of people who have engaged with similar content in the past.

The algorithm then measures how that group responds. The metrics it weights most heavily are:

**Completion rate.** What percentage of viewers watch to the end, or rewatch. A high completion rate tells the algorithm the content is compelling enough to hold attention all the way through.

**Shares to DMs.** When someone sends a video to a specific person, that's a strong signal. It means the content was valuable enough that they wanted a particular individual to see it, which requires active effort.

**Profile visits.** When a viewer is interested enough to go to your profile after watching, the algorithm reads that as the content generating genuine interest in the creator rather than just passive entertainment.

**Comments that require effort.** A comment saying "this" or "same" is a weaker signal than a comment that asks a question or starts a debate. The algorithm tracks comment quality, not just volume.

If the first cohort responds well, the video goes to a larger cohort. Then a larger one. The videos you see with millions of views got there through multiple rounds of this process, each one with a progressively bigger audience.

If the first cohort doesn't respond well, the video essentially stops. This is why you can have 200,000 followers and get 600 views on a video: the initial cohort responded poorly and the distribution stopped there.

## What changed in 2026

The algorithm has been shifting toward search-based discovery for the past two years, and in 2026 that shift is significant enough to change how brands should be thinking about content.

TikTok now functions as a search engine for a large portion of its user base, particularly for research and discovery in health, beauty, food, finance, and lifestyle. [Studies on search behaviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Search_engine) show that search intent users are more likely to convert than passive scroll users because they have a specific need they're trying to solve.

The algorithm now surfaces search-optimised content not just in search results but in the For You feed, giving higher initial distribution to videos that use search-friendly language in captions, on-screen text, and the first few seconds of audio. This means the same video can be discovered both by someone searching for a term and by someone scrolling their feed who fits the profile of someone who searches for that term.

For brands, this creates a compounding opportunity. A video that answers a common search query keeps getting discovered for months. It works as both feed content and search content. The brands building libraries of this type of content are accumulating distribution that never stops.

## The things that don't matter as much as people think

**Posting time.** The "best time to post" advice has always been overstated. Because distribution is cohort-based, a video posted at 3am that performs well in its initial cohort will still get pushed. The optimal posting time on TikTok is less than 30 minutes before your primary audience is most active, but the effect of timing on overall performance is small compared to content quality.

**Trending audio.** In 2022 and 2023, trending sounds could provide an initial distribution boost. That advantage has shrunk considerably. The algorithm has deprioritised sound as a distribution signal and shifted toward content quality signals. Using a trending sound with weak content still underperforms. Using an original sound with strong content still outperforms. The sound is no longer doing meaningful work for your distribution.

**Follower count.** Follower count does not gate distribution. An account with 500 followers and genuinely compelling content can and does outperform accounts with 500,000 followers whose content gets a poor initial response. This is the most counterintuitive thing about TikTok for brands coming from Instagram, where follower count still matters considerably.

## What this means in practice: viral content and search content are different jobs

The mistake most brands make is treating all their TikTok content the same way. Either everything is optimised for reach, or everything is optimised for search. The brands doing this well treat them as two distinct layers of the same funnel.

**Viral content sits at the top.** Its job is to bring people in who have never heard of you. It earns attention in the first three seconds, holds it to the end, and gives people a reason to share it or come back to your profile. This content is broad enough to reach beyond your existing audience, opinionated enough to stop the scroll, and memorable enough that someone who watches it knows exactly what your brand is about. It does not need to convert. It needs to pull people into your world.

**Search content sits underneath it and does the converting.** Once someone has seen your viral content and visited your profile, or found you through a search they were already running, they are in a completely different mindset. They are looking for something specific. Search content meets them there. It uses the exact language people type into TikTok when they are trying to solve a problem in your category. It answers specific questions thoroughly. It shows up consistently in the results for terms your potential customers are already searching.

The funnel works like this: viral content introduces someone to your brand and earns their curiosity. Search content is waiting for them when that curiosity turns into a question. By the time they find your search content, you are not a cold brand. You are the brand they already know, now giving them exactly what they went looking for.

Five posts a week is a reasonable floor for a brand building from scratch, with a mix of both layers. The algorithm needs enough volume to categorise your account accurately and enough variety to understand what kinds of cohorts respond to you.

The brands that treat TikTok as a hack to be gamed are the ones perpetually starting over. The brands building both layers consistently are the ones where the algorithm becomes a compounding asset rather than a lottery.
