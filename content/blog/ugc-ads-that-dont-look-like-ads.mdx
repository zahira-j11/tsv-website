The most common complaint we hear about UGC ads isn't the video quality. It's that they all start to look the same. A marketing lead at a fintech brand told us about their last batch: "Some of the hooks were very similar, so there was not much variety." Their other request was just as telling: "It should be a creator who is good on camera."

Both problems are fixable before anyone presses record. Good UGC ads are planned, cast and scripted to feel unplanned.

## Why most UGC ads underperform

- **The hooks only change by a word or two.** Ten versions of the same opening is one test, not ten.
- **The creator isn't comfortable on camera.** Viewers can tell within a second, and they scroll.
- **The ad looks like an ad.** Product held up to the lens, a list of features, a hard sell in the first line.
- **Refreshes are too slow.** By the time new creative arrives, the last batch has worn out. We cover that in [how often to refresh paid social creative](/blog/paid-social-creative-refresh).

## Start with why people don't buy

Before we write a hook, we map what stops your audience buying and what moves them. The frictions are things like upfront cost, "future me will sort it out" optimism, scepticism about whether it works, or simply too many options. The levers are things like loss aversion, a surprising result, social comparison or making the next step feel simple.

Each ad picks one friction and one lever. That gives the script a job to do, and it's why our ads follow the same shape: hook, problem, the bias that moves people, then the call to action.

## Change the hook by type, not by wording

When we write hook variations, we rotate between types of hook, so every version is a real test:

- **Curiosity gap:** the viewer has to keep watching to find out.
- **Problem first:** start with the frustration your audience already feels.
- **Benefit first:** lead with the outcome.
- **Social proof:** someone they'd trust told them about it. "My mum told me about..." works because it's how people actually find things.

We typically write two hooks per asset, pitched with the concept. The opening has to earn the stop in about two and a half seconds on a paid ad, so it's where most of the testing happens. There's more on structure in [UGC hooks](/docs/ugc-hooks).

## Vary who the ad is talking to

The same message lands differently depending on who seems to be saying it. We vary the social framing between versions:

- **First person:** "I'm a 26-year-old who..."
- **Third person:** "My best friend told me..."
- **A specific group:** one version for city workers, another for a particular age group or life stage.
- **A moment in time:** a seasonal trigger, payday, January, back to school.

Run those side by side and you learn who the message really works for, which is more useful than knowing which wording won.

## Make it feel like a FaceTime with a friend

The delivery matters as much as the script. A creator doing their skincare, putting on make-up or cooking dinner while they talk feels like a friend catching you up, not an advert. That's the feeling that stops the scroll.

It only works if the creator is good on camera. We cast from a network of 300+ vetted UK creators and put forward more options than you need, with profiles and a self-tape, so you approve who's on screen before we film. More on that in [how we cast creators](/docs/creators).

## Plan the variations before the shoot

Every concept is planned as a set: hook variations, framing variations, and every aspect ratio you'll run (9:16, 4:5, 1:1 and 16:9). One filming session then produces a full test set, rather than one ad and a promise of edits later.

## Quick answers

**How do you make UGC ads that don't look like ads?**
Start from why people don't buy, change the hook by type rather than wording, vary who the ad seems to be talking to, and have a creator deliver it while doing something everyday, so it feels like a friend talking.

**What are the best hooks for UGC ads?**
Rotate between curiosity-gap, problem-first, benefit-first and social-proof hooks. Testing different types of hook tells you far more than testing small wording changes.

**How many hook variations should a UGC ad have?**
We typically write two hooks per asset and plan more variations through framing and aspect ratios, so one shoot produces a full test set.

**What's the difference between UGC and produced paid social ads?**
UGC is creators filming themselves, usually talking to camera at home. Produced paid social ads are filmed by a crew with actors and locations, for formats like scripted street interviews and podcast-style ads.
